## What JuryHour does

You paste a landing page URL (or your pitch). An AI agent reads it, researches the market, and writes a challenger version. Real people who match your buyer, recruited and identity-verified through Terac, see both versions in random order and say which one they'd act on and why. You get a written verdict with their quotes as evidence. It is directional signal from real people, not a sales guarantee.

## Why zero employees

- The idea: every step from payment to panel to verdict is done by software and agents. Humans appear only as paid jurors.
- The /ops dashboard counts human interventions. The goal is for that number to stay at zero, and to be honest when it isn't.

## The rule that made it safe: agents decide, code enforces

- Agents choose *what* to do: the rewrite, which juror answers are low quality, whether a refund policy applies, a price within a band.
- Code enforces the *limits*: money, margin floor, panel size, rate limits, state transitions, one support refund per order.
- No agent can move money directly. Refunds go through a checked API that refuses anything outside the written policy.

## The agents

- Researcher: reads the page, researches competitors, writes the challenger card.
- Procurement: shrinks or broadens the panel when recruiting is too expensive, or refunds.
- Analyst: writes the report. A checker rejects it unless every quote is verbatim from a stored answer and every number matches the database.
- Support, Disputes, CEO, CFO and Growth handle the rest of the company.

## The stack

- Whop for payments, refunds and disputes. Terac for recruiting jurors. Exa for reading pages and research.
- Claude agents for research, report writing and operations; OpenAI models for the free preview, the safety screen and grading answers.
- Next.js on Vercel, Supabase for the database, Resend for email, Cloudflare Turnstile for bot checks.
